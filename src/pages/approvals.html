Three things wait for a person, and each is decided by somebody other than whoever asked for it:

  • An action on another platform, asked for on a case.
  • A run an automation held, because its mode is ask.
  • A privileged change: making somebody an administrator, issuing an administrator's token, weakening a setting, lowering how many approvals a change needs, or moving the audit log.
aertun approvals
aertun incident act-approve ID N
aertun automate approve RUN
aertun pending approve ID

What was asked for, by whom and why is shown before anybody decides, and the decision is in the audit log.

When you are the only administrator

A privileged change asked for by the only administrator is slowed, not blocked:

  • It needs a reason, given with the command's own option (--note on a grant, --why on an administrator's token or an audit log move, --accept-risk on a weaker setting) or written on the screen. The reason is kept in the request and in its audit record, because nobody else's approval stands beside it.
  • It waits an hour — a day to move the audit log or pass on ownership — and Aertun's detections over its own audit log raise it. Any administrator's session can decline it while it waits.
  • Approving your own request needs a passkey sign-in from the last five minutes. The People screen asks for your passkey when your sign-in is older; a command-line token is not a passkey sign-in, so aertun pending approve points you to the screen for your own requests.

The record says the change was approved alone.
