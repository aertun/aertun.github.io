Open source · self-hosted · one binary, no dependencies

The proving ground for AI security work

Aertun checks what AI defenders produce — detection rules today, triage verdicts next — against evidence the AI did not write, and signs the result in open formats anybody can verify: proven, refuted, or inconclusive. It replays attacks on AI agents through your detections, and runs on a complete security-operations core: collection, detection, an AI analyst that only suggests, and a tamper-evident record.

Built so that nobody, and no model, acts alone

AI that cannot be talked into anything

The AI analyst's plan is fixed in code before it reads a log line, its answer is one word from a closed set, and the reason a person reads is built from counts, so text an attacker wrote never reaches the screen of whoever decides.

Two people for what matters

An action on another platform is approved by somebody other than whoever asked, and making an administrator, issuing an administrator's token or weakening a setting takes a second administrator — or, for an only administrator, a delay anybody can stop. Agents propose; people decide.

A record nobody can quietly change

Every change is in an append-only audit log: a hash chain, tree heads signed with Ed25519 and ML-DSA, and the head committed to Bitcoin.
